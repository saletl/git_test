# Design review: can a child play without reading?

For every child-facing screen, the question was: "Could a child understand what to tap next without reading a single word?" Text on these screens is for grown-ups and screen readers only. This is a design review by the builder; it is not a substitute for watching real children play.

## Welcome

- **What to tap:** a big round green button with a white "play" triangle, the symbol children know from videos. After a few seconds it does one gentle pulse.
- **Bonus:** the four creatures stand in front of a little clinic. Tapping one makes it giggle and bounce.
- **Words present:** the title, tagline, and credit. None are needed to start.

## Waiting room

- **Choosing a patient:** each creature sits on a chair doing its thing (Pip blows a bubble, Waddles sneezes, Prickle peeks, Mochi's tummy rumbles), one at a time. Tapping any creature makes it hop, the door opens, and it walks through.
- **First-time help:** if nobody taps for 5 seconds, a cartoon hand appears over Pip and taps twice. This happens only once per visit to the site.
- **Surprises:** bell (hamster pops up), plant (dances), clock (bird peeks out), fish tank (fish follows your finger), magazine (cover changes). None are required.
- **Stopping:** the waving hand at bottom left, always in the same place.
- **The door:** tapping the exam-room door shows the pointing hand over a patient, so tapping the obvious "way in" still leads somewhere.
- **Result:** passes.

## Exam room

- **Tools:** four large pictures of real tools (stethoscope, thermometer, ear light, bandages) and a heart for comfort. A picked-up tool lifts and the spot where it goes glows with a dashed ring.
- **Using a tool:** tap the tool, then tap the patient anywhere, or drag the tool onto the patient. Missing the spot is fine; the tool goes to the right place.
- **First-time help:** after the patient's introduction, if nobody taps, a stethoscope floats from its button to the patient's chest and back, with a pointing hand. Later, if still idle, the stethoscope button lifts gently twice.
- **Did it work?** Every use shows the tool resting on the patient, a face change, an animation, and usually a picture (heartbeat heart, thermometer gauge, magnified ear view).
- **Feelings:** shown by faces and body language: Pip's dramatic lean, Prickle curling up and peeking from behind the bunny, a quick small heartbeat when Prickle is nervous and a slow big one after comfort.
- **Comfort choices:** pictures of the patient's own toy, music notes, an open hand, and the toy with a tool on it. Each choice gets a visible response.
- **Finishing:** after three interactions, a pink button with a heart and check mark pops in. The patient also does a happy jump when its silly problem is solved.
- **Result:** passes.

## Visit complete

- The patient waves goodbye in its own way, a sticker pops up and flies into the book icon at the top, then two big picture buttons appear: a door (next patient) and a book (stickers). Nothing starts by itself.
- **Result:** passes.

## Sticker book

- Earned stickers sit in a pocket at the bottom; empty spots on the page have dotted circles. Tap a sticker (it lifts), then tap a dotted circle (it sticks with a little bounce). A hand shows this once if needed.
- Missing stickers are pale empty circles, so the book still looks complete with one sticker.
- **Back:** the arrow at top left. **Stop:** the waving hand at bottom left.
- **Result:** passes.

## Goodbye

- A sunny sky, a rainbow, the patient (or everyone) waving, and one big green check button.
- **Result:** passes.

## Things that rely on a grown-up

- The grown-up area is meant to require reading.
- Turning the phone upright is shown with a picture of a phone rotating.
