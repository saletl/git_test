# Design review: can a child play without reading?

For every child-facing screen, the question was: "Could a child understand what to tap next without reading a single word?" Short words now appear under tools and choices so a grown-up can follow along at a glance, but nothing depends on them. This is a design review by the builder; it is not a substitute for watching real children play.

## Welcome

- **What to tap:** a big round green button with a white "play" triangle, the symbol children know from videos. After a few seconds it does one gentle pulse.
- **Bonus:** the four creatures stand in front of a little clinic. Tapping one makes it giggle and bounce.
- **Result:** passes.

## Waiting room

- **Choosing a patient:** each creature sits on a chair doing its thing (Pip blows a bubble, Waddles sneezes, Prickle peeks, Mochi's tummy rumbles). Tapping any creature makes it hop, the door opens, and it walks through.
- **First-time help:** if nobody taps for 5 seconds, a cartoon hand taps over Pip twice, once per visit to the site.
- **Stopping:** the waving hand at bottom left, always in the same place.
- **Result:** passes.

## Exam room

- **Why the patient is here:** the opening animation shows it (Pip hiccups bubbles, Waddles sneezes a scarf off, Prickle curls up behind the bunny, Mochi's tummy rumbles and the sock slips). The speech bubble says the same thing for a grown-up to read aloud.
- **Tools:** four pictures of real tools (stethoscope, thermometer, light, bandages) plus the patient's own toy for comfort. Picking one up lifts it, the good spots glow, and the first time a tool is used it floats to the patient and back to show how.
- **Using a tool:** tap the tool, then tap the patient, or drag the tool onto the patient. Missing the spot is fine; the tool still works. With the light, tapping near the ear looks in the ear, and tapping near the mouth makes the patient say "ahh".
- **Comfort:** the toy button opens four big pictures: the toy, a pillow with a moon, a music box, and two hands. Each one does something you can see (a hug, a slow breathing circle, a music box playing, a hand reaching in).
- **The patient's own adventures:** two or three picture buttons sit on the front of the exam table (a bubble jar, a scarf and hat, a tissue box, a bunny with a stethoscope, a toy shot, a question mark with music notes, a sock). Inside each, every choice is a big picture, and the patient reacts right away.
- **Pretend shot:** every step is a picture choice (a green arrow to get ready, an X for not today, comfort pictures, bandage patterns, the toy shot, a glowing spot on the arm). The X at the top stops it at any step. The words for grown-ups are honest: "Shots can pinch for a moment. You can ask for help."
- **Bandages:** pick a pattern, then tap anywhere on the patient. Tap a bandage to pick it up again (it gets a dashed ring), then tap a new spot; or drag it. The curved arrow undoes, the trash can removes.
- **The chart:** a clipboard picture with three dots that fill in as the child plays. Inside, three circles light up with pictures (a heart, bubbles, a bow tie, a tissue box, a bunny, a sock...). The big stamp button finishes the visit with a satisfying thunk. Nothing needs to be filled first.
- **Talk-together ideas:** an occasional yellow speech bubble with two small speech icons. It never blocks play and disappears on its own or with a tap.
- **The room:** the lamp, poster, sink and cabinet each do something when tapped; they are the only obviously interactive objects on the walls.
- **Finishing:** after a couple of things, a pink button with a heart and check mark pops in. The chart stamp finishes too.
- **Result:** passes. The sound mystery is the activity that most benefits from sound; with sound off, the matching toy still glows after two guesses and the thought bubble shows the answer.

## Visit complete

- The patient waves goodbye in its own way, the new coloring page pops up and flies into the book icon, then two big picture buttons appear: a door (next patient) and a book. Nothing starts by itself.
- **Result:** passes.

## Coloring book

- Exactly four cards. Earned pages show the black-and-white picture; the others show a faded picture of the patient to visit. Tap a page to see it large; the arrow at top left goes back.
- **Result:** passes.

## Goodbye

- A sunny sky, a rainbow, the patient waving, and one big green check button.
- **Result:** passes.

## Things that rely on a grown-up

- The grown-up area is meant to require reading.
- Turning the phone upright is shown with a picture of a phone rotating.
